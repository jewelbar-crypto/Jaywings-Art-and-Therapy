What is Art Therapy

Could incorporating art help my therapeutic journey?

About Art Therapy

Art therapy uses creative processes alongside talking therapy to support exploration, self-expression, and meaning-making. Artistic skill or experience is not required. The emphasis is on process rather than outcome, and sessions may involve art-making, image-based reflection, or non-verbal exploration adapted to the individual.

Benefits of Art Therapy

Creative processes can support:

  • Emotional regulation and grounding
  • Mindfulness and reflection
  • Connection to identity, strengths, and values

Who Can Benefit

  • People experiencing anxiety, depression or low mood
  • Clients with trauma histories or grief
  • Migrants, people with speech/language differences, and neurodivergent clients
  • Anyone seeking deeper self-understanding or non-verbal ways to process experience

How Art Therapy Works

Some clients use art primarily for expression and reflection; others explore images and symbols in more depth as part of therapeutic work. Approaches are blended with talking therapy and guided by your goals and readiness.

Media & Modalities

A wide range of media may be used, including:

  • Drawing (pencil, charcoal), painting (watercolour, acrylic), collage and mixed media
  • Sculpture and simple modelling
  • Creative writing, photography and other expressive approaches

Participation in art-making is always optional. Sessions can be offered in person or online.

Art Therapy Online

Art therapy can be offered effectively in an online format. Clients may use materials they already have at home, work digitally, or engage in reflective and image-based processes without creating physical artwork.

Online art therapy allows for flexibility, accessibility, and continuity of care, and can be particularly helpful for clients who prefer to work from their own space.
